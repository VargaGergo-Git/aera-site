Privacy policy

Last updated November 2026

Your health data never leaves your phone.

Aera works everything out on your iPhone. There is no Aera account, and Aera's own servers never see your health data. This policy covers what does leave, and why.

Summary

  • Health data stays hereRead from Apple Health and worked out on this phone. Never sent.
  • Usage counts, if you say yesWhich screens you open and what you tap, under a random code.
  • Crash reportsSent when something fails, so it gets fixed. No health data.
  • Maps see an area, not youMap services get the place you look at, never your name.
  • Purchases go through AppleAera never sees your card or your Apple ID.
  • Your rightsSee, erase or object at any time. Just write.

Who is responsible

Aera is made by Gergo Varga, 8000 Székesfehérvár, Nagyszombati utca 156., Hungary, who is responsible for your data ("we"). Write to Aerahealthapp@gmail.com, or use Contact in Settings.

What stays on your iPhone

Your health values, scores, workouts, sleep, notes and your name. Aera reads Apple Health only with your permission, which you can change any time in iOS Settings › Privacy & Security › Health. We never receive any of it.

What leaves, and where

  • Usage counts go to Mixpanel in the EU, only if you say yes. In the UK and Switzerland they start on, with a notice, until you turn them off.
  • Crash reports go to Sentry in the EU.
  • Map lookups go to Apple Maps, OpenStreetMap servers, OpenFreeMap, Wikipedia and Amazon Web Services.
  • Workout weather is looked up with Apple Weather.
  • Names you give places go to your own iCloud.
  • Linked devices, like Oura or Polar, send your data to this phone, only if you link them.
  • Purchases go through Apple.

Usage counts

Off until you say yes in the EU, the US, Canada, Australia and most other countries. In the UK and Switzerland they start on, with a notice. Turn them off any time in Settings › Privacy. If what Aera counts grows, it asks you again.

What is sent:

  • which screens you open and what you tap
  • which settings you change, never their values
  • how far setup got and how fast the app opens
  • how bigger steps ended, like saving a route or an import
  • app version, device model, iOS version, region setting, Premium status and a random install code

Every value is a fixed word or a rounded number. Never health data, scores, routes, names, anything you type, your email or an advertising ID. Mixpanel is told not to use your IP address for location. Turning counts off deletes anything not yet sent and retires the code.

Earlier versions also sent rough score bands and feedback categories. Those records stay until they expire or you ask us to erase them.

Legal basis: your consent; in the UK and Switzerland, our legitimate interest in improving Aera. Kept for up to 24 months.

Crash reports

When Aera crashes, freezes or a step fails, it sends Sentry a report: what failed, app and iOS version, device model, language and region settings, memory and battery state, the last steps in the app, and a random crash code. Never screenshots, screen content, your name, health data or GPS. Turn them off any time in Settings › Privacy.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in an app that works. Kept for up to 90 days.

Maps and places

To draw maps, name places and fetch weather, Aera sends the coordinates of the place involved: the area you look at, or where a workout happened. Never your name, health data or a code that identifies you.

Like any website, these services see your IP address. Some, such as Apple and Wikimedia, are in the US. The names you give places and routes are backed up to your own iCloud with a simplified outline, so reinstalling keeps them. Turn off iCloud for Aera to stop this.

Legal basis: providing the features you use.

Linked devices

If you link a device account, like Oura or Polar, you sign in on the maker's own page. Aera's small sign-in helper at Cloudflare swaps that sign-in for a key and hands it to this phone. It never sees your health data, and Aera logs and keeps nothing there. Aera then downloads your data straight to this phone. Nothing is sent back. The key stays in this phone's keychain, and unlinking deletes it. The device maker's own policy covers your account with them.

Purchases

Apple takes the payment. Aera only learns which plan is active, never your card, name or Apple ID. When paid plans return, Apple will also report each purchase to RevenueCat (US), our subscription service: plan, price, country, dates and a random purchase code.

Legal basis: your purchase. Kept while your subscription runs and as long as accounting law requires.

No ads, no tracking

We don't sell data, show ads or track you across apps and websites.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct, erase or download your data, limit its use, or object to it. You can withdraw consent any time, which doesn't affect what was sent before. Write to us. Contact in Settings adds your random codes, so we can find your records. We reply within a month.

Delete all data in Settings › Privacy erases what Aera keeps on this phone, except its random codes, which stay so your records can still be found. Apple Health stays untouched.

You can also complain to a data protection authority: in Hungary the NAIH (naih.hu), or the one where you live.

Children

Aera is rated 9+. If you are under 16, ask a parent before saying yes to usage counts.

This website

aerahealth.app counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which sets no cookies, stores nothing on your device and does not track you across sites; it records the page you opened, the page that sent you, your browser type and your country.

The route planner

The route planner at aerahealth.app/plan works inside your browser. The points you place and the route it finds are never sent to Aera.

The map and the paths come from OpenFreeMap, a free map service built on OpenStreetMap data. Your browser downloads map tiles straight from OpenFreeMap, which receives your IP address and the areas of the map you load, as any website you visit does.

When you send a route to your iPhone, the QR code holds a link with your points written after the # sign. Browsers don't send that part when they open the page, so it never reaches Aera or OpenFreeMap. If you download a GPX file, your browser saves it on your computer.

If you press the locate button, your browser asks before sharing your location, and the planner uses it only to move the map. This page has no Cloudflare visit counter, because that counter reads the whole address, including the part after the #.

Changes

When this policy changes, the new version shows here with its date. Where your consent is needed, Aera asks again.
