Why resting heart rate rises

2026-09-11

Your resting heart rate is up when your overnight low sits above your own usual range, not when it fails a friend's number. One high morning is usually ordinary noise. A run of high mornings is the nudge worth a second look.

Resting heart rate is beats per minute when you are doing nothing. Your heart is calmest in sleep, so the overnight floor is steadier than anything you catch standing in the kitchen at noon. The morning number on a watch is usually that quiet floor, not a daytime snapshot.

What lower usually means

Reimers and colleagues (2018) reviewed interventional studies on exercise and resting heart rate. Across that evidence, endurance training over weeks and months tends to drift resting rate down. A fitter heart moves more blood per beat, so it can settle slower at rest. A drop of a few beats across a training block is a real change, and usually a good one.

PatternHonest read
One high morningUsually noise: late meal, drink, hard session, short night, heat, stress
Several mornings above your rangeWorth noticing; can be early illness or piled load
Slow drop across a training blockOften fitness settling in (Reimers et al., 2018)

Why one spike is rarely a story

Plenty of ordinary things lift the overnight floor for a night. A late or heavy meal. A drink. A hard session the day before. A short or broken night. Heat, dehydration, caffeine, or a stressful day. Any one of these can show up higher the next morning. One high reading on its own rarely means a thing.

Mishra and colleagues (2020) showed that smartwatch resting-rate rises can appear before someone feels sick, in the specific setting of pre-symptomatic COVID-19 detection from wearable data. That is a research finding about early signal in groups, not a home test. A sustained climb above your normal can be one of the earliest hints that something is brewing. It is still not a diagnosis.

Your baseline, not anyone else's

Normal resting rates vary widely between healthy people. Your friend's number tells you almost nothing about yours. This week against your usual range is the comparison that means something.

Resting rate and overnight variability often move in opposite directions under load. When illness, sleep debt, training, or alcohol pile up, resting rate tends to rise while What is HRV? tends to fall. Reading them together is clearer than either alone. What the watch can and cannot say

Your watch can tell you that resting rate is up. It cannot tell you why. Late dinner, a drink, a tough workout, a poor night, heat, stress, and an illness coming on can all produce the same rise.

Zhang and colleagues (2016) meta-analysed resting heart rate against all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in the general population. Ho and colleagues (2014), using Framingham data, reported long-term cardiovascular risks associated with an elevated heart rate. Both are broad patterns in groups. They are not a verdict on your morning. I have not opened the full texts for exact risk ratios here, so I am not attaching a personal hazard number to a wrist reading.

An elevated reading is not a diagnosis. A low rate usually reflects fitness, yet a watch cannot tell healthy athletic slowness from the rare cases where a slow rate is a problem. Read it as how today compares to your own normal. Anything unusual that comes with symptoms is a conversation for a clinician, not an app.
