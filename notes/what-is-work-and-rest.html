Work and rest

2026-09-24

Work and rest rhythm is the rise and fall of your heart rate when a session has no GPS route to read, like lifting or a circuit.

A lift has no kilometres. It still has a pulse that climbs on the push and eases on the rest. When that rise and fall is clear, the heart trace can be read as a rhythm. Label it as an estimate. Never treat it as a grade of how hard you trained.

Reading a session with no route

When there is no track to split, the honest signal left is the effort itself. Walk the heart-rate trace. Find the moments it rose into a hard push, and the moments it fell back in the rest. Each detected effort is one column in that story. How tall it is follows how high the heart climbed. The cool stretch after it is the settle before the next one.

One useful summary is how many beats the heart eased, on average, in the half-minute after each detected effort. A bigger number describes a larger drop in that session. It does not grade the set. It does not promise you were ready for the next one.

It needs a real rhythm to read. A steady, unbroken effort has no pushes and rests to find. Stay quiet unless the session clearly rose and fell.

Why the settle between efforts matters

The instant you stop pushing, a calming branch of your nervous system, the vagus nerve, reaches back in and pulls the heart rate down. Imai and colleagues (1994) showed that this early, vagally mediated drop is accelerated in athletes. How fast the rate falls in the first minute is one of the most studied signs of a well-trained heart. People whose heart rate drops quickly after effort tend to have stronger, more responsive hearts.

Cole and colleagues (1999) measured how far the heart drops immediately after exercise ends. That is the famous first-minute test, taken in a controlled setting after hard work. Stanley and colleagues (2013) reviewed how the calming side of the heart comes back after exercise, and why that return matters when you prescribe the next bout. Daanen and colleagues (2012) reviewed heart-rate recovery as a way athletes watch training status over time.

The settle between your sets is a looser, noisier cousin of that first-minute test. Effort, rest length, heat, position, and sensor quality all move it. One workout is never a verdict on conditioning. Across genuinely similar sessions, the pattern can still be useful context. The controlled post-exercise drop is a different question. That lives in How fast should my heart settle after exercise?

Three kinds of rhythm

The same heart trace can describe three different sessions. The difference is how completely you settle between pushes, not how impressive a single peak looks.

RhythmWhat the heart usually does
StrengthHeavy pushes with real rest; long, deep settles before the next set
IntervalsAn even back-and-forth; the heart rises and falls in a steady wave
ConditioningShort rests that keep the heart working; it barely settles, which is the point

None of those shapes is a better grade. A deep settle on a strength day is the design. A shallow settle on a conditioning day is also the design. Read the rhythm against the session you meant to do.

Outdoor interval work is usually a different read. The watch already has steps. You do not need to infer them from the pulse. That lives in What are interval workouts?

The honest limits

Heart rate is a rough guide for lifting. A heavy, low-rep set can be brutally hard yet barely lift your heart rate, because the strain is on the muscle, not the engine. So read this as the shape and rhythm of the session. Never as a grade of how hard you trained. How the session felt is a separate question. That lives in What is workout effort?

The famous recovery test is a controlled minute after all-out exercise. The settle between sets is not that test. Treat it as a within-person pattern to watch over time, not a clinical score to compare against anyone else.

A session with no clear rise and fall has no rhythm to name. Saying nothing is better than inventing sets the heart never showed.

When the heart rose and fell, you can name the rhythm. You still cannot grade the work. That is the line worth remembering.
