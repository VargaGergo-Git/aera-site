Field note

VO2max on a watch

2026-09-17

VO2max on a watch is an estimate of the most oxygen your body can put to work during hard exercise. Of everything a consumer watch reads, cardiorespiratory fitness is one of the strongest long-run health signals, and the digit itself is still only an estimate.

If you only remember one sentence: trust the monthly trend from outdoor cardio, not the exact number on any one day.

What the number means

VO2max is the largest amount of oxygen your body can pull in and use during hard effort. Picture a steep hill that keeps going. The fitter you are, the longer you can climb before your body runs out of room. That ceiling is what this number captures.

Oxygen has a long journey through your body. Your lungs take it in, your heart pumps it, your blood carries it, and your muscles burn it. VO2max is the one number that shows how well that whole chain works together. When any link gets stronger, the number rises. It is the closest thing your watch has to a whole-body fitness score.

Why it carries weight

Kodama and colleagues (2009) meta-analyzed cardiorespiratory fitness as a quantitative predictor of all-cause mortality and cardiovascular events in healthy men and women. Mandsager and colleagues (2018) associated higher fitness with lower long-term mortality among adults undergoing treadmill testing. As fitness goes up, the odds keep improving in these population studies. There is no clean point where the association seems to stop.

Left alone, VO2max drifts down a little each year once you pass your thirties. A higher value means your heart and lungs are working more like a younger version of themselves. That is why fitness often leads longevity-style reads on a watch. Why is my resting heart rate up? is a quieter overnight floor. VO2max is closer to your ceiling.

How it climbs

This number rewards patience, not heroics. Huang and colleagues (2005) meta-analyzed controlled endurance training and VO2max changes in older adults. Steady, easy-to-moderate cardio over months is what moves it. Days do not change it. Seasons do.

Many watches only refresh the estimate from outdoor cardio with GPS. A brisk walk counts when you can still talk but would rather not sing. Indoor and strength workouts often will not refresh the number. A few sessions a week, kept up over months, does more than one hard week.

What is training strain? places today's heart-rate load next to your recent weeks. That context helps you build the outdoor base without treating one hard day as the whole fitness story. What does a readiness score mean? is the morning-capacity sibling when you are deciding whether to push.

What the watch can and cannot say

Your watch does not measure VO2max directly. A lab does that with a mask and gas analysis. The watch estimates it by watching how your heart rate responds to pace outdoors, then blending in age, height, sex, and weight. Wrist estimates often read a little below a lab value, so a rising line is the useful signal.

Because updates often need qualifying outdoor cardio, the number can sit still for someone who mainly trains indoors, lifts, or cycles. That is a measurement gap, not proof your fitness stopped. The longevity link is a strong association in groups. The watch cannot prove that raising your number adds years, and it cannot diagnose any heart or lung condition.
